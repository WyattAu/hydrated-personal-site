# The November 19 2026 G1 storm, captured by this site's own monitor

Answer: Kp reached 5.0 at 00:00 UTC and SWPC issued a K05A alert at 01:08
UTC. The site's aurora monitor — which had been writing a 30-minute
probability series to storage all along — recorded the whole envelope:
baseline 20%, peak 55% at 02:30 UTC, and a 0.79 Pearson correlation
against the official Kp index. This is the first storm the site has
captured with its own instrumentation.

## The captured envelope

- Baseline (before 23:00 UTC Nov 18): 20% average
- Steepest ramp: 32% → 50% between 23:30 and 00:30 UTC
- ≥40% readings: 00:00–03:30 UTC (42, 50, 51, 48, 49, 55, 54, 46)
- Peak: 55% at 02:30 UTC
- Elevated (≥20%) for roughly 16 hours; back to 14–15% baseline by 13:00 UTC

## SWPC alert timeline (Nov 19 UTC)

- 00:41 K04W — WARNING: K-index 4 expected
- 00:46 K04A — ALERT: K-index 4
- 01:00 K05W — WARNING: K-index 5 expected
- 01:08 K05A — ALERT: K-index 5 (G1 minor storm threshold)
- 01:18 K04W — Warning Condition: Persistence

The monitor's steepest ramp preceded the formal K05A alert by about
40 minutes — finer resolution than the 3-hour Kp index can express.

## Correlation against the official index

Pearson r = 0.787 over 45 aligned points (site probability vs NOAA
planetary K-index, same 24h window). Previous windows were too quiet to
compute this honestly — this storm is the first event with enough
dynamic range to validate the monitor against the official index.

## Methodology

Sources: this site's 48-point aurora history (30-min cadence, Cloudflare
KV, cron-written) and NOAA SWPC products. The correlation forward-fills
the 3-hour Kp onto the 30-minute grid; unweighted Pearson. This page is
a fixed snapshot of a past event — that is what makes it a record.
